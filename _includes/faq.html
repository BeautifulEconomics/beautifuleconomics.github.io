Why Beautiful Economics?

Because most economics writing picks one of two bad options. It either flatters you with a simple story that falls apart the moment you press on it, or it hides behind notation that takes a semester to decode. Both leave you unable to think with the idea afterwards.

The word beautiful is not decoration. A good economic idea has the same quality as a good proof: a few plain assumptions, followed carefully, arriving somewhere you did not expect and cannot now unsee. Comparative advantage is beautiful in exactly that way. So is the reason a shared field gets stripped bare while a private one does not.

So every entry here says three things: what the idea claims, what it quietly assumes, and where it stops working. That last part is the one most explanations skip, and it is the part that turns a definition into something you can actually use.

No account, no fee, no algorithm deciding what you see. Every page keeps its own permanent address, so what you learn here stays where you can find it again.
